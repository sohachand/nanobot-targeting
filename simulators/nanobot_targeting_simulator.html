How the simulation works

Each patient file holds the tumor that the U-Net segmented on every MRI slice. The simulator stacks those slices into a 3D volume and sends three nanobots from the same entry point to the tumor, each with a different navigation strategy.

01MRI slicesFLAIR brain slices from the LGG dataset, resized to 256 × 256.
02U-Net segmentationPer-pixel tumor probability, thresholded at 0.5 into a binary mask.
03Centroid extractionImage moments give each slice’s tumor centre (x, y) and area.
043D target pathCentroids ordered by slice index form the tumor’s path through the volume.
05Nanobot navigationThree pursuit strategies move toward the target, 1.5 voxels per step.

Navigation strategies

Static target

Moves in a straight line to the mean of all tumor centroids. It is the shortest route, but it ends at the tumor’s average position rather than on tissue that was actually segmented.

p ← p + s · (t − p) / |t − p|

Dynamic tracking

Visits each slice’s centroid in order and moves on once within 1 voxel. It follows the tumor’s real shape, at the cost of a longer path.

for each c[i]: pursue c[i] until |c[i] − p| < 1

Obstacle-aware

Dynamic tracking plus a repulsive field around critical structures. The repulsion fades as the bot nears its waypoint, which stops it getting trapped next to an obstacle.

u = dir(t) + k · f · Σ (p − o) / |p − o|²

Comparison metrics

Path length
Total distance travelled, in voxels.
Steps
Number of moves until the strategy finishes.
Final distance
Distance from the end point to the nearest segmented centroid.
Slices visited
Tumor slices the bot passed within 1 voxel of.
Min clearance
Closest approach to a critical structure’s surface. A negative value means the path entered it.

Critical structures are simulated: three spheres placed along the entry-to-tumor route to test avoidance. They are not segmented from the MRI.
